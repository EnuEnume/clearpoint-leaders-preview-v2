The moment

When your usual way of deciding stops working.

Professional growth, a business exit, a change of leadership role, or a partnership conflict can all disrupt the reference points you're used to relying on.

The weight of the decision is still yours, and the people around you need you steady — so there is rarely a confidential space to think without pressure, and to notice the recurring patterns in how you think and interact before they raise the cost of the decision.

This is not weakness. It is the cost of carrying a system on your shoulders. What you need now is not advice, and not another framework — it is an external, unhurried perspective on the situation you're in.

A confidential space to regain clarity and decide deliberately.

Working method

An authored three-stage structure
— not a clinical protocol.

A structured, individual process grounded in transactional analysis and in individual work with clients since 2021. This is my own three-stage structure for consultative work — it is not a validated or standardised clinical protocol.

01

Assessing the current situation

We clarify the request and the actual situation: what's happening, what decisions have already been made, what resources have been lost or retained, and the emotional and interpersonal factors involved.

02

Defining the desired change

We define the outcome you want, the criteria for reaching it, the risks and constraints, and the sequence of steps. The pace of the work is agreed with you and matched to the real conditions of the situation.

03

Forming an autonomous position

You define a decision that fits the current reality, your own values, and the limits of your responsibility — and plan how to sustain it under difficult conditions.

The shift

What you can work on
over three months.

Possible areas of work, not a guaranteed effect. Specific goals and criteria for the result are agreed individually at the start of the work.

A clearer decision process

You separate facts from assumptions and emotional reactions, and set clear criteria for the decision in front of you.

A clearer view of the problem

You structure the situation and identify the one or two factors that actually move it.

Awareness of recurring patterns

You recognise repeating patterns in how you lead and relate that may be sharpening conflict or weakening decisions.

A realistic plan and ways to sustain it

You leave with a realistic plan of next steps, and ways to support the decision after the work ends.
